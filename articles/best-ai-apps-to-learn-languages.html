7 Best AI Apps to Learn Any Language in 2026 (Free & Paid)

✍️ AIGuideDZ Team📅 Updated September 2026📖 8 min read

The hardest parts of learning a language — getting speaking practice, instant corrections, and staying consistent — are exactly what AI is best at. It doesn't matter whether you're learning English, Spanish, Japanese, or German: an AI tutor never gets tired, never judges your accent, and costs a fraction of private lessons (often nothing).

Here are the seven tools that actually move the needle, whatever language you're learning, plus a couple of bonus picks if English specifically is your target.

1. ChatGPT Voice — best free speaking partner, any language

Best for: conversation practice, any level, any language. Price: free tier available.

Voice mode turns ChatGPT into a conversation partner you can talk to in your target language about anything, anytime. The trick is to give it a role first: "You're my [language] conversation partner. Speak a bit slower than normal, and correct my important mistakes at the end of the conversation." Ten minutes a day of real speaking beats an hour of passive grammar drills — in any language.

2. Claude — best writing coach, any language

Best for: improving your written skills in your target language. Price: free tier available.

Paste anything you write — emails, essays, messages, in whatever language you're learning — and ask Claude to correct it and explain each correction. The explanations are the point: you learn the rule, not just the fix. Try: "Correct this [language] text. For each change, show before → after and a one-line explanation of the rule." Our prompts library has ready-made prompts you can adapt to any language.

3. Duolingo — best for building a daily habit, 40+ languages

Best for: beginners and consistency, in almost any language you can name. Price: free with ads.

Duolingo won't make you fluent on its own, but its streak system solves the #1 reason learners fail: quitting. Its AI features add conversation practice and smarter review, and it covers everything from Spanish and French to Japanese, Korean, and dozens more. Use it as your daily minimum — the habit anchor — and do your real practice with the other tools here.

4. Google Translate Conversation Mode — best live two-way crutch

Best for: real conversations with a native speaker before you're ready to go solo. Price: free.

Conversation mode listens to both sides of a spoken exchange and translates in real time — genuinely useful for travel, or for early-stage practice with a native-speaking friend or tutor when you both need a safety net. It's not a substitute for actually learning, but it removes the fear that stops people from trying to speak at all.

5. YouGlish — best for hearing real-world speech in context

Best for: hearing how words are actually used by native speakers. Price: free.

Type any word or phrase and YouGlish plays YouTube clips of real people saying it in context — thousands of examples across accents. It covers English, French, Spanish, German, Italian, Portuguese, Russian, Arabic, and more. Perfect for answering "do people actually say this?" and for training your ear on natural speed and rhythm.

6. Speechling — best AI + human pronunciation coaching

Best for: fixing your accent across multiple languages. Price: free tier, paid for full coaching.

Speechling combines AI feedback with real human coaches who review your recordings — a genuinely useful hybrid for the languages it supports (including Spanish, French, German, Japanese, Korean, and Mandarin, alongside English). If accent and pronunciation are your bottleneck, this closes the gap that self-study alone struggles with.

7. Anki with AI-generated decks — best for vocabulary retention

Best for: actually remembering the words you learn. Price: free (desktop & Android), paid iOS app.

Anki's spaced-repetition algorithm is the most research-backed way to make vocabulary stick — the gap is always building good decks. Ask ChatGPT or Claude to generate a deck: "Create 30 flashcards for [language] beginner vocabulary about [topic], format: front / back / example sentence." Import it, and you've turned an hour of prep into five minutes.

A 30-minute daily routine that actually works

  1. 5 min — Duolingo. Keep the streak; warm up.
  2. 10 min — Speak with ChatGPT Voice. One topic per day (yesterday, your plans, an opinion). Ask for corrections at the end.
  3. 10 min — Write with Claude. A short paragraph on the same topic; get corrections with explanations. Keep a note of recurring mistakes.
  4. 5 min — YouGlish or your Anki deck. Drill the words you stumbled on today.

The sequence matters: speaking and writing about the same topic doubles the reinforcement. After 30 days you'll have a personal error list — that list is your real curriculum, in whatever language you're building it for.

💡 Tip: Set your phone's system language to your target language too. Free immersion, all day, zero effort.

🏆 Our verdict

Start free: ChatGPT Voice for speaking + Claude for writing + YouGlish for listening covers all core skills at zero cost, in almost any language. Add Speechling if pronunciation is your bottleneck, or ELSA/Speak specifically for English.
