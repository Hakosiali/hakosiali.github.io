7 Best AI Note-Taking Apps in 2026 (Free Tiers Compared)

✍️ AIGuideDZ Team📅 Updated July 2026📖 10 min read

Note-taking apps used to compete on folders and fonts. Now they compete on what happens after you write: auto-summaries, transcribed lectures, flashcards generated from your own notes, and answers pulled from everything you've ever captured. Here are the seven worth your time, judged the way we always judge tools — by what the free tier actually gives you.

  1. NotebookLM — best for studying
  2. Notion — best all-in-one workspace
  3. Obsidian — best for privacy & power users
  4. Otter — best for lectures & meetings
  5. OneNote + Copilot — best free canvas
  6. Goodnotes — best for handwriting

1. NotebookLM — best for students, full stop

Google's NotebookLM flips note-taking around: instead of writing notes, you upload sources — lecture slides, PDFs, articles, YouTube links — and it becomes an expert on your materials. Ask questions, generate study guides, create quizzes, or listen to a podcast-style audio overview of the week's readings. Because it only answers from your sources, invented facts are rare.

Free tier: generous and fully usable. Weakness: it's not where you jot quick thoughts — pair it with any simple notes app. Full walkthrough: our NotebookLM study guide.

2. Notion — best all-in-one workspace

Notes, tasks, databases, wikis — Notion is where many people run their whole life, and Notion AI sits across all of it: summarize a messy page, draft from an outline, auto-fill database properties, and answer questions from everything in your workspace. Free tier: excellent for personal use; AI features are a paid add-on. Worth paying for only if Notion is already your home base.

3. Obsidian — best for privacy and power users

Your notes are plain Markdown files on your own device — no account, no cloud requirement, free for personal use. AI arrives via community plugins that connect local or cloud models, so you choose what leaves your machine. The linked-notes graph is unmatched for building a long-term knowledge base. Trade-off: a real learning curve. Students who invest a weekend rarely go back.

4. Otter — best for recorded lectures & meetings

Otter records, transcribes in real time, identifies speakers, and produces summaries with action items. For lectures (where recording is permitted) and group project calls, it means actually listening instead of scribbling. Free tier: monthly transcription minutes that cover a few lectures a week. Always ask permission before recording people — in many jurisdictions it's the law.

5. OneNote + Copilot — best free infinite canvas

OneNote remains completely free with the best freeform canvas in the business — type anywhere, draw, clip, mix handwriting and text. Microsoft's Copilot adds summarization and drafting if you're in that ecosystem. If your school gives you Microsoft 365, this is the zero-cost default that most people underrate.

6. Goodnotes — best for handwriting on a tablet

If you think with a stylus, Goodnotes turns handwritten notes into searchable, AI-summarizable text, fixes your scrawl's spelling, and can generate practice questions from your notebooks. Pricing: limited free version; one-time or subscription unlock. The handwriting-recognition quality is what you're paying for.

7. Reflect & Mem — best auto-organization

Both bet on the same idea: stop filing notes, let AI surface them. Everything goes in one stream; AI links related notes, resurfaces them when relevant, and answers questions across your entire history. Both are paid products aimed at professionals drowning in meetings and ideas. Try them if folder systems keep collapsing under you.
