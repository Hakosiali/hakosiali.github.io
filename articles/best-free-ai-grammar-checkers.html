7 Best Free AI Grammar Checkers in 2026 (Grammarly Alternatives)

✍️ AIGuideDZ Team📅 Updated September 2026📖 8 min read

Grammar checkers stopped being spellcheck-with-extra-steps a while ago. The good ones now catch tone, wordiness, clarity and passive voice — not just typos — and several genuinely useful ones cost nothing. Here are seven worth using, judged by what the free tier actually unlocks.

📑 The tools
  1. Grammarly — the standard, still solid free
  2. QuillBot — best for rewriting, not just fixing
  3. ProWritingAid — best for long-form writing
  4. Hemingway Editor — best for clarity & readability
  5. LanguageTool — best open-source, privacy-friendly pick
  6. ChatGPT — best free-form editing partner
  7. Google Docs — best zero-setup option

1. Grammarly — the default, and still a solid free tier

Grammarly's browser extension and desktop app check spelling, grammar, and basic clarity anywhere you type — email, Google Docs, social media. The free tier is genuinely usable daily; it just won't touch tone rewrites, advanced fluency suggestions, or plagiarism checks, which sit behind Grammarly Premium. Free tier: generous for everyday writing. Weakness: the upsell prompts get repetitive.

2. QuillBot — best for rewriting, not just correcting

QuillBot leads with its paraphraser: paste a clunky sentence and get several rewritten versions in different tones (formal, simple, creative). Its free grammar checker is solid too, and the combination — fix, then rewrite for clarity — is genuinely different from a pure error-catcher. Free tier: word-count-limited paraphrasing, unlimited basic grammar checks. Best for: turning a rough draft into something readable, not just correct.

3. ProWritingAid — best for essays and long-form writing

Where Grammarly optimizes for quick everyday writing, ProWritingAid is built for longer documents — essays, reports, even novels. Its free reports break down overused words, sentence-length variation, passive voice, and readability with more depth than most competitors. Free tier: full reports with a word-count cap per check. Best for: a research paper or thesis you'll revise more than once.

4. Hemingway Editor — best for cutting the fluff

Hemingway doesn't check grammar so much as force clarity: it flags passive voice, adverbs, and sentences so dense they need a second read, then gives your writing a readability grade. The web app is completely free with no account required. Weakness: no login means no cross-device sync, and it won't catch subtle grammar errors the way Grammarly does. Best for: a final clarity pass before you submit anything.

5. LanguageTool — best open-source, privacy-conscious pick

LanguageTool is open-source, supports over 20 languages, and — unlike most competitors — offers a genuinely capable free tier without constant upsells. It integrates with Google Docs, Word, and browsers just like Grammarly. Best for: anyone writing in more than one language, or who'd rather not hand an American company every draft they write.

6. ChatGPT — best free-form editing partner

Paste a paragraph and ask ChatGPT to "fix grammar without changing my voice," or "explain every change you'd make and why." It won't underline errors inline like a dedicated checker, but it explains why something's wrong — genuinely useful if you're trying to actually improve, not just submit. Full walkthrough: our ChatGPT beginner's guide.

7. Google Docs — best zero-setup option

If you already write in Google Docs, its built-in spelling and grammar suggestions (Tools → Check grammar) catch more than people expect, entirely free, with zero extensions or accounts to manage. It won't rewrite for tone or flag wordiness like the dedicated tools above — but for a quick pass on something already in Docs, it's the fastest option that exists.
