7 Best AI Apps for Math & Homework Help in 2026

✍️ AIGuideDZ Team📅 Updated July 2026📖 9 min read

Point a phone camera at a math problem and get a full step-by-step solution in seconds — that's not a novelty anymore, it's the baseline. The real question is which app explains why instead of just handing over an answer, because copying the final number teaches you nothing before the next exam. Here are the seven worth having installed.

One ground rule before the list: use these to check your work and understand steps you got stuck on — not to skip doing the problem yourself. Teachers can usually tell the difference between a student who learned the method and one who only has the answer.

  2. ChatGPT — best all-subject helper
  3. Claude — best for showing full reasoning
  4. Gemini (Lens) — best for mixed photo homework

1. Photomath — best camera scanner

Point your camera at a printed or handwritten problem and Photomath recognizes it instantly and shows a full step-by-step solution, often with multiple solving methods to choose from. Covers arithmetic through calculus. Free tier: covers basic step-by-step solutions; a paid tier unlocks deeper explanations and textbook-specific walkthroughs. Best for: the "I know the answer's wrong somewhere but can't find where" moment.

2. ChatGPT — best all-subject helper

Beyond math, ChatGPT helps with the science, history and language-arts homework that dedicated math apps don't touch, and its vision feature reads photos of handwritten work directly. Ask it to solve and explain, one step at a time, waiting for you to confirm each step before continuing. New to it? Start with our ChatGPT beginner's guide.

Copy-paste prompt: "Solve this step by step, but stop after each step and ask me to predict the next one before you reveal it: [paste or describe problem]."

3. Claude — best for showing full reasoning

For word problems and multi-step algebra where the reasoning matters more than the arithmetic, Claude tends to lay out its logic more carefully and is less likely to skip a step you needed explained. It also handles long, multi-part assignments pasted in full without losing track of which sub-question is which. See ChatGPT vs Claude for the full comparison.

4. Gemini (Lens) — best for mixed photo homework

Google Lens, built into Gemini, recognizes math problems, diagrams and even messy handwriting in a photo, and Gemini explains the solution conversationally. Handy because the same app also answers the science and geography questions on the same worksheet. New to Gemini? See our Gemini beginner's guide.

5. Mathway — best for advanced/college math

Covers everything from basic algebra to statistics, calculus and linear algebra, with a solver built specifically for higher math notation. Free tier reality: gives you the final answer; the step-by-step breakdown is behind a subscription. Best used alongside a free chatbot: get the answer here, the explanation there.

6. Khan Academy (Khanmigo) — best structured tutoring

Unlike a general chatbot, Khanmigo is built around Khan Academy's actual curriculum — it won't just solve a problem, it guides you through the concept with Socratic questioning, and tracks what you've mastered over time. Free tier: the core video lessons and practice have always been free; Khanmigo AI features have expanded free access for students in many regions. The best option if you want genuine tutoring, not just an answer key.

7. Symbolab — best for seeing multiple solution methods

Where other tools show a method, Symbolab often shows several ways to solve the same problem side by side — useful when your teacher wants a specific technique (e.g., factoring vs. the quadratic formula). Strong graphing tools too. Free tier: limited daily solutions with steps; unlimited requires a subscription.

💡 The habit that actually improves your grade: after any tool solves a problem, close the app and redo it from scratch on paper without looking. If you can't, you didn't learn the step — you copied it. That five-minute check is the difference between these tools helping you and quietly making you worse at math.
