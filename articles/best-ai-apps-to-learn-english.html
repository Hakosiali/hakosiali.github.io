7 Best AI Apps to Learn English in 2026 (Free & Paid)

✍️ AIGuideDZ Team📅 Updated July 2026📖 8 min read

The hardest parts of learning English — getting speaking practice, instant corrections, and staying consistent — are exactly what AI is best at. An AI tutor never gets tired, never judges your accent, and costs a fraction of private lessons (often nothing).

Here are the seven apps that actually move the needle, based on what each does best.

1. ChatGPT Voice — best free speaking partner

Best for: conversation practice, any level. Price: free tier available.

Voice mode turns ChatGPT into a conversation partner you can talk to about anything, anytime. The trick is to give it a role first: "You're my English conversation partner. Speak a bit slower than normal, and correct my important mistakes at the end of the conversation." Ten minutes a day of real speaking beats an hour of passive grammar drills.

2. Claude — best writing coach

Best for: improving your written English. Price: free tier available.

Paste anything you write — emails, essays, messages — and ask Claude to correct it and explain each correction. The explanations are the point: you learn the rule, not just the fix. Try: "Correct this text. For each change, show before → after and a one-line explanation of the rule." Our prompts library has five ready-made English-learning prompts.

3. Duolingo — best for building a daily habit

Best for: beginners and consistency. Price: free with ads.

Duolingo won't make you fluent, but its streak system solves the #1 reason learners fail: quitting. Its AI features add conversation practice and smarter review. Use it as your daily minimum — the habit anchor — and do your real practice with the other tools here.

4. ELSA Speak — best for pronunciation

Best for: fixing your accent and being understood. Price: free tier, paid for full course.

ELSA listens to you speak and scores each sound, showing exactly which ones betray your accent — the feedback is startlingly precise. If people ask you to repeat yourself in English, two weeks of ELSA drills will do more than a year of listening practice.

5. Speak — best structured speaking course

Best for: intermediate learners who want a curriculum. Price: paid, with trial.

Speak is built around talking from minute one: you speak your answers, and its AI tutor role-plays real scenarios (interviews, travel, small talk) with corrections. It's the closest app experience to having a private tutor on a schedule.

6. Grammarly — best everyday corrector

Best for: catching mistakes in everything you type. Price: free tier.

Install the browser extension and every email, comment and document you write gets checked in real time. For learners, the value is passive: seeing your own recurring mistakes flagged daily is spaced repetition for grammar, without opening a textbook.

7. YouGlish — best for real-world listening

Best for: hearing how words are actually used. Price: free.

Type any word or phrase and YouGlish plays YouTube clips of real people saying it in context — thousands of examples across accents. Perfect for answering "do people actually say this?" and for training your ear on natural speed and rhythm.

A 30-minute daily routine that actually works

  1. 5 min — Duolingo. Keep the streak; warm up.
  2. 10 min — Speak with ChatGPT Voice. One topic per day (yesterday, your plans, an opinion). Ask for corrections at the end.
  3. 10 min — Write with Claude. A short paragraph on the same topic; get corrections with explanations. Keep a note of recurring mistakes.
  4. 5 min — ELSA or YouGlish. Drill the words you stumbled on today.

The sequence matters: speaking and writing about the same topic doubles the reinforcement. After 30 days you'll have a personal error list — that list is your real curriculum.

💡 Tip: Set your phone's language to English too. Free immersion, all day, zero effort.

🏆 Our verdict

Start free: ChatGPT Voice for speaking + Claude for writing + YouGlish for listening covers all core skills at zero cost. Add ELSA if pronunciation is your bottleneck.
