How to Cite ChatGPT and AI Tools in MLA, APA & Chicago (2026)

✍️ AIGuideDZ Team📅 Updated September 2026📖 7 min read

If you used ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or any other AI tool while researching or writing, most style guides now expect you to say so — the same way you'd cite an interview, a database, or a piece of software. Here's the exact format for MLA 9, APA 7, and Chicago, with copy-paste examples for each.

⚠️ Check your instructor's policy first. Some courses ban AI assistance entirely, some require disclosure without a formal citation, and some want a full citation like any other source. These formats assume citation is required or allowed — always confirm the rule for your specific class or publication.

MLA 9 format

MLA's official guidance treats the AI tool as a container with no listed author. Describe your prompt in the text, then cite the response as the "title."

Works Cited entry:

"Describe or summarize your prompt here." ChatGPT, version used (e.g. GPT-4), OpenAI, [date accessed], chat.openai.com/chat.

Example:

"Explain the causes of the 2008 financial crisis in three paragraphs." ChatGPT, GPT-4, OpenAI, 11 Sept. 2026, chat.openai.com/chat.

In-text citation: since there's no author or page number, MLA recommends introducing the AI output in your own sentence rather than using a parenthetical citation — e.g. "When asked to explain the causes of the crisis, ChatGPT generated a summary that highlighted subprime mortgages and deregulation."

APA 7 format

APA's official guidance treats the company behind the tool as the author, and recommends reproducing long AI-generated text as a block quote since there's no retrievable, stable "page" to cite.

Reference list entry:

OpenAI. (2026). ChatGPT (GPT-4) [Large language model]. https://chat.openai.com/chat

In-text citation:

(OpenAI, 2026)

APA also recommends describing how you used the tool in your methods section or a footnote, and — where required by your institution — appending the full prompt and response as an appendix.

Chicago format

Chicago's guidance (17th edition) treats AI-generated text similarly to unpublished or personal communication — typically cited in a footnote rather than the bibliography, since a chat response isn't a retrievable published source.

Footnote:

1. ChatGPT, response to "Describe your prompt here," OpenAI, September 11, 2026.

If your instructor wants a bibliography entry as well, use the same author-title-date structure as the footnote, moving the date to the end.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to cite ChatGPT if I only used it for brainstorming?

Check your instructor's or publication's policy first — many only require a citation when AI-generated text, ideas, or structure appear in your final work, not for brainstorming you didn't use directly. When in doubt, cite it or add a brief note describing how you used it.

What's the difference between citing ChatGPT in MLA vs APA?

MLA 9 treats the AI chatbot as a container in a Works Cited entry with no author, using "ChatGPT" as the title-like element. APA 7 treats the AI company (e.g. OpenAI) as the author in both the in-text citation and reference list entry.

Do I need to include the exact prompt I used?

MLA recommends describing or quoting your prompt in the text or a note. APA doesn't require the prompt in the reference entry, but recommends including it in the text or an appendix if it's relevant to understanding your work.

How do I cite Claude, Gemini or another AI tool instead of ChatGPT?

Use the same template and just swap in the tool's name and company: Anthropic for Claude, Google for Gemini. The structure — company as author, tool name, version/date, and URL — stays the same across MLA, APA and Chicago.
