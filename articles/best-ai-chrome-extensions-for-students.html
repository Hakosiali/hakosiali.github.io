8 Best AI Chrome Extensions for Students in 2026 (Free)

✍️ AIGuideDZ Team📅 Updated September 2026📖 8 min read

The fastest way to use AI as a student isn't another app to open — it's a browser extension that's already there when you're reading a PDF, stuck on a long article, or staring at a blank Google Doc. Here are eight worth installing, all with usable free tiers.

📑 The extensions
  1. Grammarly — grammar everywhere you type
  2. Merlin — ChatGPT on any webpage
  3. LanguageTool — multilingual grammar
  4. Sider — AI sidebar for any tab
  5. Glasp — highlight & summarize the web
  6. Monica — all-in-one AI assistant
  7. Youtube & Article Summary — skip the fluff
  8. Wiseone — research reading companion

1. Grammarly — grammar checking everywhere you type

Once installed, Grammarly quietly checks spelling, grammar, and basic clarity in Google Docs, Gmail, Canvas, and pretty much any text box in your browser. The free tier is genuinely usable daily. Full comparison of alternatives: our AI grammar checker roundup.

2. Merlin — ChatGPT-4 access on any webpage

Merlin puts a GPT-4-powered assistant one keyboard shortcut away on any site — summarize the page you're on, ask questions about a PDF, or draft an email reply without switching tabs. Free tier: a daily query limit that covers light use. Best for: research where you don't want to keep copy-pasting into a separate ChatGPT tab.

3. LanguageTool — grammar for 20+ languages

If Grammarly's English-first focus doesn't fit your coursework, LanguageTool is open-source, supports over 20 languages, and offers a capable free tier without constant premium nudges. Works in Docs, Gmail, and most text fields the same way Grammarly does.

4. Sider — an AI sidebar for every tab

Sider opens a persistent chat sidebar next to whatever page you're on, with access to multiple models (including GPT-4 and Claude on free/low-cost tiers) and one-click actions like "summarize," "translate," or "explain." Best for: people who want one AI panel instead of ten different single-purpose extensions.

5. Glasp — highlight, save, and summarize the web

Glasp lets you highlight text on any page the way you'd highlight a textbook, then generates AI summaries of long articles or YouTube transcripts from what you've saved. Over time it builds a personal, searchable library of everything you've read and highlighted. Free tier: fully usable for personal study.

6. Monica — all-in-one AI assistant extension

Monica bundles chat, writing, summarizing, and translation into a single extension with a generous free daily quota across multiple AI models. It's a reasonable one-extension answer if you don't want to manage separate tools for separate tasks.

7. YouTube & Article Summary — skip straight to what matters

Several free extensions (search "AI summary" in the Chrome Web Store) generate a timestamped, skimmable summary of any YouTube video or long article in seconds — genuinely useful for deciding whether a 40-minute lecture recording is worth watching in full before an exam.

8. Wiseone — a reading companion for research

Wiseone highlights key facts as you read, lets you ask questions about the article inline, and cross-checks claims against other sources — closer to a research assistant than a summarizer. Best for: literature reviews and research papers where you need to actually understand, not just skim.
