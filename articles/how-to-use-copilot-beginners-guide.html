How to Use Microsoft Copilot: Complete Beginner's Guide (2026)

✍️ AIGuideDZ Team📅 Updated September 2026📖 8 min read

If your school or job gives you a Microsoft account, you may already have access to a capable AI assistant and not know it. Copilot is Microsoft's answer to ChatGPT — built into Windows, Bing, and the Office apps you probably already use. Here's what it actually does and how to start.

What Copilot actually is

Copilot is Microsoft's umbrella brand for AI across its products — a standalone chatbot at copilot.microsoft.com (powered by OpenAI's models, the same family behind ChatGPT), built into Bing search, baked into Windows 11 via a taskbar icon, and layered into Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and Outlook for anyone with the right Microsoft 365 plan. The short version: if you just want a free chatbot, copilot.microsoft.com works like ChatGPT with no account required for basic use.

Free Copilot vs Copilot Pro

The free web and Windows version covers everyday chat, web-grounded answers with citations, and basic image generation — genuinely usable daily, no subscription needed. Copilot Pro ($20/month) adds priority access during peak times, more image generations, and — critically — Copilot integrated directly into Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and Outlook, rather than just the standalone chat.

Important nuance: some schools and employers provide Microsoft 365 Copilot through their organization's license at no extra cost to you — check with your institution's IT department before assuming you need to pay.

Where you'll find it

  • copilot.microsoft.com — the standalone web chat, works in any browser, free tier available without sign-in for basic chat.
  • Windows 11 taskbar — a Copilot icon opens a sidebar version, useful for quick questions without switching windows.
  • Bing search — Copilot answers appear alongside regular search results.
  • Microsoft 365 apps — Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, and Teams, if your plan includes it.

Copilot in Word, Excel & Outlook

This is where Copilot earns its keep for students and office workers specifically:

  • Word: draft a document from a prompt, rewrite a paragraph's tone, or summarize a long document you're reviewing.
  • Excel: describe a formula in plain English, get a chart from raw data, or ask it to explain what a spreadsheet is actually doing. Full walkthrough: our AI in Excel guide.
  • Outlook: draft email replies from a few bullet points, or summarize a long thread you were CC'd into.
  • PowerPoint: generate a first-draft deck from a Word document or prompt — see also our AI presentation maker roundup for dedicated alternatives.

Copilot vs ChatGPT vs Gemini

Functionally, Copilot's underlying chat model is closely related to ChatGPT's — so day-to-day answer quality is broadly comparable. The real differentiator is ecosystem: Copilot wins if you already live in Word, Excel, and Outlook; Gemini wins if you're on Google Docs and Gmail; ChatGPT remains the most flexible standalone option with the broadest plugin and app ecosystem. See our full ChatGPT vs Gemini comparison for the Google side of that decision.

💡 Quick start: if you already have a Microsoft/school account, open copilot.microsoft.com and just start typing — no separate signup required. Check your school or employer's Microsoft 365 plan before paying for Copilot Pro yourself.
