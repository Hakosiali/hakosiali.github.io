How to Write Better AI Prompts: Beginner's Guide to Prompt Engineering

✍️ AIGuideDZ Team📅 Updated July 2026📖 11 min read

Two people ask an AI the same question. One gets a generic paragraph; the other gets exactly what they needed, formatted and ready to use. The difference isn't the AI — it's the prompt. The good news: prompting well is a skill you can learn in an afternoon, and it works the same in ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini.

This guide gives you the formula, seven techniques with before/after examples, and the mistakes that quietly ruin your results.

The 4-part prompt formula

Almost every great prompt contains the same four ingredients. Memorize this and you're ahead of 90% of users:

  1. Role — who the AI should act as. "You are an experienced study coach…"
  2. Task — what exactly you want, with specifics. "…create a 2-week revision plan for my biology exam…"
  3. Context — what the AI needs to know. "…I have 90 minutes per day, these 6 chapters, and I struggle with genetics…"
  4. Format — how the answer should look. "…as a day-by-day table with a daily 10-minute review task."
💡 Before/after: "Help me revise biology" → generic advice. The full 4-part version above → a personalized, usable plan on the first try. Same AI, same effort, completely different result.

7 techniques that instantly improve your results

1. Show an example (few-shot prompting)

If you want a specific style, paste an example of it. "Here's an email I wrote that has the tone I want: [paste]. Write a new email to a professor asking for a deadline extension in the same tone." Examples beat descriptions every time.

2. Give it an out

Tell the AI it's allowed to say it doesn't know: "If you're not sure, say so instead of guessing." This measurably cuts down made-up facts — essential for research and citations.

3. Ask for structure before content

For anything long (essay, report, presentation), ask for an outline first, edit the outline, then ask it to write. You'll steer the result with far less rewriting.

4. Set constraints

Word counts, reading level, what to exclude: "Under 200 words. No jargon. Don't mention pricing." Constraints are the difference between a draft you use and a draft you delete.

5. Iterate — don't restart

The first answer is a first draft. Reply with corrections: "Good, but make point 2 more specific and drop point 4." Each round takes seconds and compounds. Restarting from scratch throws away the context you just built.

6. Ask it to critique itself

"Review your answer above. What's weak, missing or wrong? Then give an improved version." Surprisingly effective — models are better editors than first-drafters.

7. Use "think step by step" for anything with logic

Math, planning, debugging, decisions: adding "work through this step by step before giving your final answer" makes reasoning visible so you can spot exactly where it went wrong — and it often just gets the answer right more often.

5 mistakes that ruin your results

  • Vague asks. "Make this better" forces the AI to guess what "better" means. Say what to improve: clarity? length? tone?
  • Kitchen-sink prompts. Ten instructions in one message means some get ignored. Break big jobs into steps.
  • No audience. "Explain photosynthesis" — to a 10-year-old or a university student? Always name the reader.
  • Trusting facts blindly. AI states wrong things confidently. Verify names, numbers, dates and citations before using them anywhere real.
  • One-and-done. Accepting the first answer. The best results almost always come on round two or three.

Copy-paste templates

Study: "You are a patient tutor. Explain [topic] to a [level] student using an everyday analogy, then give me 5 practice questions from easy to hard. After I answer, tell me what I got wrong and why."

Email: "Write a [formal/friendly] email to [person] about [situation]. Goal: [outcome]. Under 150 words, no filler phrases, end with a clear next step."

Summarize: "Summarize this [document] in 5 bullet points a busy manager can read in 30 seconds, then list any deadlines or action items separately: [paste]."

Decide: "I'm choosing between [A] and [B]. My priorities are [X, Y, Z]. Compare them in a table against my priorities, then recommend one and explain the trade-off I'm accepting."

FAQ

Do prompts work the same in ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini? The principles above work identically in all of them. Differences show up only at the edges — see our ChatGPT vs Claude and ChatGPT vs Gemini comparisons for which to pick.

Is "prompt engineer" a real job? As a standalone title it's fading, but prompting is now a baseline skill in marketing, development, analysis and admin roles — like Excel was in 2005.

Should I use prompt marketplaces? Rarely worth paying for. The 4-part formula plus iteration beats almost any purchased mega-prompt.
